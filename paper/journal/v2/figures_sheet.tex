\mainfig{13cm}{fig1_design}{Design of the study. (a) The ten classes of one group of the 17-class set, a resonance $X$
decaying to two quarks or gluons, drawn as schematic jets, with the class each falls in under the four label sets.
Below, all 188 classes as cells; a bar spanning several cells is one class of that label set, and the outline marks
the group drawn above. (b) One model is pretrained for each label set on the same jets and is then used with frozen
features or fine-tuned.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig2_frozen}{Linear probes on the frozen features: accuracy on (a) the 188 JetClass-II classes and (b)
JetClass, and background rejection Rej$_{30\%}$ on (c) top tagging and (d) quark/gluon discrimination. Points are
means over pretraining runs and bars standard deviations; the vertical line and band give the 188-class model's.
Bars and bands narrower than a point are hidden by it. The two accuracy panels share one scale relative to the
188-class model, as do the two rejection panels.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig3_pairs}{Background rejection against signal efficiency for two pairs of JetClass-II classes, from
binary linear probes on the frozen features: (a) $X\to b\bar b$ against $X\to c\bar c$ and (b) $X\to bc$ against
$X\to bq$ and $X\to cs$. A curve ends where fewer than 20 of the 200,000 background jets pass, so the ratio to the
188-class model (lower panels) starts where that model's curve does. Random groupings that merge the pair and those
that keep it apart are averaged separately; bands are one standard deviation.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig4_training_size}{Fine-tuned models against the number of training jets: accuracy on (a) JetClass-II
and (b) JetClass, and background rejection Rej$_{30\%}$ on (c) top tagging and (d) quark/gluon discrimination. Lines
are the mean over runs and bands the standard deviation. The same network trained from scratch is shown in grey.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig5_resonance}{Sophon's $X\to b\bar b$ and $X\to bs$ benchmark: signal efficiency against the
efficiency for QCD jets, for jets in the mass window. Solid lines are transfer learning on the frozen features and, in
grey, the same classifier trained from scratch. Dash-dotted lines in (a) are the discriminant formed directly from the
output scores, where the label set keeps $X\to b\bar b$ as a class. Bands are the standard deviation over runs.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA1}{Recall of each JetClass-II class under a linear probe on the frozen features of the (a) 162-, (b)
43- and (c) 17-class model, against its recall for the 188-class model. Points are means over runs, with bars
combining run spread and test-sample uncertainty; colors and shapes give what the panel's label set merges the class
with. Open points lie within three standard deviations of equal recall.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA2}{As figure 3, for the four other class pairs: (a) $X\to YY\to b\bar bqq$ against $c\bar cqq$, and
QCD jets (b) with $b\bar b$ against $c\bar c$, (c) with $b$ against light quarks and gluons only, (d) with $c$ and no
$b$ against light quarks and gluons only. Every random grouping merges each QCD pair, so (b) to (d) have one
random-grouping curve.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA3}{Linear probes on the frozen features against the number of training jets: accuracy on (a)
JetClass-II and (b) JetClass, and background rejection Rej$_{30\%}$ on (c) top tagging and (d) quark/gluon
discrimination. Lines are the mean over runs and bands the standard deviation.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA4}{Jet mass estimated by the models pretrained with mass regression, beside the soft-drop mass:
median response (a, b) and resolution (c, d) against jet $p_{\mathrm T}$ and against generator-level mass. Lower
panels give the distance of the median from one and the ratio of the resolution to that of the soft-drop mass. Bars
combine run spread and test-sample uncertainty.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA5}{Validation accuracy of the pretraining runs against epoch, each model on its own label set, so
levels compare only between models with the same number of classes. Lines are the mean over runs and bands the
standard deviation.}

\mainfig{15cm}{fig1_design}{The four nested label sets and how each pretrained model is used. (a) One group of the 17-class set, a
resonance $X$ decaying to two quarks or gluons: its ten classes and their classes at 188, 162, 43 and 17 classes.
(b) A Particle Transformer is pretrained on JetClass-II with one label set; a classifier is trained on its frozen
features (a linear probe, or for $X\to b\bar b$ and $X\to bs$ a small network), or the network is fine-tuned.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig2_accuracy}{Accuracy of every pretrained label set on the 188 JetClass-II classes (a) and on JetClass (b). The
upper parts give linear probes on the frozen features (open markers), the lower parts models fine-tuned on $10^6$ jets
(filled), each part with its own range. Markers and bars are means and standard deviations over runs, faint dots the
single runs and, for the random groupings, the five draws. The dashed line and band are the network trained from
scratch.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig_separation}{Frozen-feature accuracy rises with the separation a label set keeps between confusable
classes. Each point is a pretrained label set: its confusion-weighted separation $S$ against the linear-probe accuracy
on the 188 JetClass-II classes, the mean and standard deviation over runs. Over the twelve label sets the Spearman
correlation is $\rho=\RevSepRho$ ($p=\RevSepP$); weighting by the confusion of an untrained network instead gives
$\rho=\RevSepUntrainedRho$.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig3_classes}{Where the frozen-feature accuracy is lost. (a) Mean change of per-class recall under a linear probe
against the 188-class model, by what the label set merges a class with; bars are standard deviations over runs.
(b) One minus the AUC of a binary linear probe for pairs of classes, relative to the 188-class model (value in brackets);
open where the labels merge the pair, half-filled where partly. $X\to gg$ vs $q\bar q$: 5,000 test jets per class.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig5_resonance}{Sophon's resonance benchmark for $X\to b\bar b$ (a) and $X\to bs$ (b): QCD rejection against signal
efficiency in the mass window. Solid lines are a classifier trained on the frozen features of each label set and, in
grey, Sophon's network trained from scratch on the same jets; dash-dotted lines use the output scores where the label
set keeps $X\to b\bar b$. Bands: standard deviations over runs; dotted line: 1\% background efficiency.
Lower panels: ratio to 188 classes.}
\mainfig{15cm}{fig4_training_size}{Fine-tuned accuracy on JetClass-II (a) and JetClass (b) against the number of training jets, for
each label set and for the network trained from scratch. Lines and bands are means and standard deviations over runs
(Table D4). Lower panels give each label set's difference to the 188-class model in percentage points, with both run
spreads combined.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA1}{Recall of each JetClass-II class under a linear probe on the frozen features of the (a) 162-, (b) 43-
and (c) 17-class model, against its recall for the 188-class model, colored by what the label set merges the class
with; faded points lie within three standard deviations of equal recall. Points are means over runs with bars
combining run spread and test-sample uncertainty.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA2}{Background rejection against signal efficiency for three pairs of resonance classes, from binary linear
probes on the frozen features, for the four label sets and the random groupings that merge the pair or keep it apart.
Every curve is drawn from the signal efficiency below which fewer than 20 background jets pass the 188-class model.
Lower panels give the ratio to the 188-class model; bands are standard deviations over runs.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA6}{As figure A2, for three pairs of QCD classes. Every random grouping merges these pairs.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA3}{Linear probes on the frozen features against the number of training jets: accuracy on JetClass-II
(a) and JetClass (b), background rejection Rej$_{30\%}$ on top tagging (c) and quark/gluon discrimination (d). Lower
panels give the difference to the 188-class model in percentage points (a, b) or the ratio to it (c, d). Lines and
bands are means and standard deviations over runs.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA7}{As figure 5, for top tagging (a) and quark/gluon discrimination (b): background rejection
Rej$_{30\%}$ of fine-tuned models against the number of training jets. Lower panels give the ratio to the 188-class
model.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA4}{Jet mass estimated by the models pretrained with mass regression, beside the soft-drop mass:
median response (a, b) and resolution (c, d) against jet $p_{\mathrm T}$ and against generator-level mass. Lower
panels give the distance of the median from one and the ratio of the resolution to that of the soft-drop mass. Bars
combine run spread and test-sample uncertainty.}
\mainfig{15cm}{figA5}{Validation accuracy of the pretraining runs against epoch, each model on its own label set, so levels
compare only between models with the same number of classes. Lines are the mean over runs and bands the standard
deviation; the shading marks the last ten epochs, among which each run's checkpoint is the one of highest validation
accuracy (ticks).}

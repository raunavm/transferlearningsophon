\begin{abstract}
Jet foundation models are pretrained on simulated collision data with labels
whose granularity is chosen by convention rather than measured. Two axes of
pretraining design have recently been ablated in controlled fashion --- the
training objective and the composition of the pretraining data --- but the
label space itself has not. We pretrain Particle Transformers on JetClass-II
under a nested contraction of its \Nnative{} native labels, holding the data
stream bit-identical across arms so that the label vocabulary is the only
variable. We report two findings. First, collapsing a physical distinction out
of the pretraining vocabulary degrades that distinction in the frozen
representation, and a nonlinear probe does not recover it --- the information is
destroyed rather than merely rendered nonlinear. Second, and independently of
the first, cross-entropy is not comparable across label spaces, so
loss-to-downstream scaling laws for particle-physics foundation models are
silently conditioned on a fixed vocabulary. We state what the study does not
yet establish, and pre-register the control that would separate \emph{how many}
distinctions a vocabulary keeps from \emph{which} ones.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Foundation models for jet physics are now trained at a scale where the
pretraining recipe, not the architecture, is the object of study. Two design
axes have recently been ablated under controlled conditions. \citet{objectives}
compare pretraining \emph{objectives} --- supervised classification,
flow-matching generation, and masked particle modelling --- and find that pure
classifier pretraining is optimal when downstream labels and model capacity are
plentiful. \citet{datacomp} vary the \emph{composition} of the pretraining
data, showing that scaling behaviour can be engineered by making the
pretraining corpus more diverse and better aligned with the downstream task.

A third axis has not been ablated: the \emph{label space}. If supervised
classification is the best pretraining objective, then the vocabulary of that
classification is the design choice that matters most, and it is currently set
by convention. Production taggers have grown from tens to hundreds of classes
without a controlled measurement of what that buys. The question is not
academic, because the vision literature disagrees with itself about the answer:
\citet{huh} found that \emph{coarsening} ImageNet helped transfer to few-class
targets, \citet{hong} found finer labels monotonically better on ImageNet21k
but U-shaped on iNaturalist, and \citet{mehta} report representations
saturating at roughly eight categories.

We contribute a controlled instrument for that question in jet physics. Our
ladder holds the training stream bit-identical across arms, so the vocabulary
is the only variable --- a control that none of the vision studies above could
apply, because coarsening an image taxonomy changes which images are sampled.

\section{A controlled granularity ladder}

\paragraph{Data and vocabulary.} JetClass-II labels each jet with one of
\Nnative{} native classes: \Nresonant{} resonant decay signatures and \Nqcd{}
QCD categories. We define a nested contraction tree over these labels and study
two rungs of it here: \textbf{L162}, which merges only the QCD block
($K=162$), and \textbf{R16\_Q1}, which merges the resonant signatures into
16 topological groups plus one QCD node ($K=17$). The map is materialised, not
regenerated from a seed, so every arm sees an identical label array.

\paragraph{What is held fixed.} All arms use the same Particle Transformer
\citep{part} with \Nparams{} trainable parameters, the same
$\Nexamples$ examples seen over \Nepochs{} epochs, and the same learning rate
$\LR$. Crucially, the flat $(p_T, m_\mathrm{SD})$ reweighting histograms that
determine \emph{which jets are drawn and in what order} are verified
byte-identical across arms, so the arms differ in their loss function and in
nothing else. Selection retains \Ntrainjets{} training and \Ntestjets{} test
jets.

\paragraph{One learning rate is defensible.} A separate rate per arm would
confound granularity with tuning. We swept three rates per arm at 20\% budget.
The seed spread at a fixed rate is \tiemargin{} in validation accuracy, which
sets the resolution. At $\LR$, R16\_Q1 attains its outright optimum
(\RsixteenLRb) and L162 is \lrgap{} below its own (\LsixtwoLRb{} against
\LsixtwoLRc{}) --- one fifth of the noise floor, i.e.\ a tie. A single rate
therefore serves both arms.

\paragraph{Endpoint.} Arms with different $K$ emit incommensurable logits, so
the comparison is made on the frozen 128-dimensional representation the
classifier head reads, probed on tasks defined on the \emph{native} labels and
therefore identical for every arm. We fit both a linear and a nonlinear (MLP)
probe. The linear probe lower-bounds the mutual information, so a linear null
alone cannot distinguish an absent distinction from a present but nonlinearly
encoded one; we therefore never report one without the other.

\section{Results}

\subsection{A collapsed distinction is destroyed, not merely hidden}

A single contrast on a collapsed axis cannot support the claim, because a
coarser vocabulary might simply yield a weaker representation of everything. We
therefore probe three axes chosen to form a $2\times2$, and read the arms
against each other \emph{within} an axis rather than across them.

\textbf{bvc\_resonant} holds topology fixed and varies flavour:
$X\!\to\!b\bar b$ against $X\!\to\!c\bar c$, both two-prong hadronic. L162
resolves it; R16\_Q1 collapses it. \textbf{retained\_topology} is its
complement --- it holds flavour fixed (both endpoints all-$b$) and varies prong
count: $X\!\to\!b\bar b$ against $X\!\to\!YY\!\to\!b\bar bb\bar b$. Both arms
resolve it. \textbf{bvc\_qcd} is the null-versus-null leg, collapsed by both
arms, where neither should win.

The design turns a single comparison into a difference-in-differences. A gap
confined to the axis R16\_Q1 uniquely collapses, with the retained axis and the
null-versus-null leg both at parity, is not explicable by a globally weaker
representation. Difficulty is not matched across the three axes and cannot be:
R16\_Q1 groups by topology, so every distinction it retains is topological.
This is why the arms are compared only within an axis.

The direction on the collapsed axis is expected; the magnitude and the
\emph{nonlinear} result are not. If the collapsed distinction were merely
absent from the linear readout, an MLP probe should recover it --- $b$ and $c$
information is present in the inputs and remains useful to classes the coarse
vocabulary retains. It does not recover it. We therefore read the effect as
degradation of the represented information rather than a change of coordinates.
A linear probe lower-bounds the mutual information, so this distinction between
``absent'' and ``present but nonlinear'' is exactly what the second probe buys.

\subsection{Cross-entropy is not comparable across label spaces}

\citet{scaling} fit a joint model-and-data scaling law for collider-jet
transformers and connect it downstream, reporting that lower pretraining loss
yields systematically lower fine-tuning loss and higher background rejection.
That mapping is established within a fixed vocabulary. Our ladder shows the
condition is load-bearing and unstated.

Cross-entropy over 17 classes and over 162 classes are not the same quantity, so
a loss-to-downstream forecast is silently conditioned on a fixed label space.
The empirical ordering makes the point concrete and survives the obvious
remedy: R16\_Q1 has the lower loss under both the raw comparison and entropy
normalisation (Table~\ref{tab:loss}), while transferring worse on the axis it
erased (Table~\ref{tab:probe}). No monotone rescaling of the loss axis recovers
the ordering. We read this as a constraint on the domain of validity of
loss-based compute allocation, not as a refutation of it.

\paragraph{Pipeline validation.} Under an identical recipe on JetClass-I our
pretrained arm reaches macro AUC \ePartOurs{} against the published \ePartPub{}
for the same architecture \citep{part}, so the comparison above is not an
artefact of the training path.

\section{What this does not show}

\paragraph{One pretraining seed on the fine arm.} The paired bootstrap
resamples jets, not runs, and carries no pretraining-seed component. Our
measured seed variance is $\sigma=\sigmapretrain$ in $\log_{10}(1-\mathrm{AUC})$
on a from-scratch arm, and the coarse arm's own spread across seeds is
\vbSeedSpread. Interval statements here are conditional on a single fine-arm
pretraining run.

\paragraph{How many, or which?} The central limitation. Our coarse arm reduces
$K$ from 162 to 17 \emph{and} erases specific physical distinctions, and the
present design cannot separate the two. The discriminating experiment is a
semantics-matched random control: a pretraining arm at matched $K=17$ whose
partition of the \Nnative{} native labels is random rather than physical. If it
degrades the probed axis as much as the physical contraction does, then only
capacity matters; if the physical contraction is worse, then \emph{which}
distinctions a vocabulary keeps matters beyond \emph{how many}. We pre-register
this arm and the prediction that the physical contraction will be worse. We
note that \citet{chen} randomise the target while holding auxiliary labels
fixed, which is the inverse manipulation.

\paragraph{Two rungs, not a curve.} \citet{mehta} report saturation near eight
categories; a ladder whose coarse endpoint is $K=17$ cannot observe that knee.

\section{Conclusion}

Holding the pretraining data stream bit-identical and varying only the label
vocabulary, we find that a physical distinction collapsed out of the
pretraining vocabulary is degraded in the frozen representation beyond what a
nonlinear probe can recover, and that pretraining loss is not a vocabulary-
independent quantity, which bounds where loss-based scaling laws may be applied.
The label space is a third axis of pretraining design alongside the objective
and the data, and it is the one currently set by convention.
